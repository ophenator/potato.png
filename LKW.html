The Phlugadör


Illustration by: Bay Bihed M.D.

As you can see in this beautiful image, the Phlugadör is a truly unique creature, with its vibrant colors and intricate patterns.
It's tongue is used to snatch birds, snakes, and small mammals out of the trees in southeast asia that it calls it's home. It's paws are an adaptation for tree-climbing, used purely for scaling trees to get to it's nest of phlugadör grubs.
It reproduces in a most peculiar way, almost reminding me of a very popular science fiction horror movie. It injects an egg into it's victim's body using it's long ovipositor, where the larva then hatches and begins attempting to escape. It then bursts out of the victim's chest, and begins singing a ragtime song while dancing with a cane and top hat.
The phlugadör's teeth are packed with a most potent nuerotoxin, one of which is capable of causing hallucinations in its predators or prey. Most organisms on the recieving end of the bite see their long lost relatives doing the hokey-pokey, but a very select few have reported seeing Elvis Presley in plaid PJs.
It's wings are most innefective. Actually, we have no idea why they even exist on the phlugadör's back!
The antennae are used for hearing only, as it has no true ears. It's 'eyes' are used for detecting heat and most varieties of fruit snack gummies in its environment.

In conclusion, the phlugadör is a most wonderful creature, but is best to be viewed from a distance.


The Three-Toed Tree Eating Malurgia


Illustration by: Art "Ificialint" Elligence

The Three-Toed Tree Eating Malurgia is a fascinating creature found in the thick woodlands often bordering creeks in the eastern United States. With its three-toed limbs and specialized diet, it has adapted to life in the trees, where it feeds on primarily lichen.
They feed on lichen, yes, but they are often found on low branches hovering over the water, as that is their preferred breeding ground and living area.
I can not give much more information about them at all, as the only source I could find even proving their existence is a single, low-quality video.

For this reason, I must conclude that the Three-Toed Tree Eating Malurgia is a creature shrouded in mystery, and further research is needed to uncover its secrets.
